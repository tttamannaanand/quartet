Chapter 1 · easiest

How much alpha survives Fama-French?

Alpha is the return a fund earns that its risk can't explain. CAPM explains returns with the market alone. Fama-French adds two more factors: SMB (small companies beat big ones) and HML (cheap "value" companies beat expensive ones). If a fund's edge was just owning those, its alpha shrinks.

Chapter 2

Calm or stressed? A hidden Markov model on the S&P 500

Each day the market is secretly in a calm or a stressed state, each with its own volatility. The model, fitted by expectation maximisation, guesses the state from returns. The shading shows its probability of stress.

SPY price, shaded by P(stressed)

Chapter 3

When should you exercise an American put?

An American put can be exercised any day until expiry. Longstaff-Schwartz simulates 100,000 price paths, then works backwards, using regression to estimate the value of waiting. Exercise when the payoff now beats it. A binomial tree checks every answer.

Chapter 4 · hardest

Heston: when volatility itself is random

Black-Scholes assumes one volatility for every option. Real prices disagree: out-of-the-money puts cost more, making a "smile". Heston lets volatility wander, with five parameters fitted to today's SPY options.
